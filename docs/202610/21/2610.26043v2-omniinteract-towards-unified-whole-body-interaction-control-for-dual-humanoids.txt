ABSTRACT
Physical interaction is a key capability for humanoid collaboration, requiring co-
ordinated whole-body motion and precise contact.
However, learning diverse
dual-humanoid interactions faces two major challenges: (1) human-robot mor-
phological differences disrupt contact interfaces and spatial relationships dur-
ing retargeting, and (2) jointly learning motor skills and partner coordination
makes it difficult to retain control fidelity as interaction diversity grows. In this
paper, we propose OmniInteract to address these challenges through surface-
aware modeling and scalable residual policy learning. First, we introduce Sur-
face Contact-Aware Retargeting, which leverages dense human-robot surface
correspondences to jointly retarget both humanoids, preserving explicit interac-
tion contact interfaces and their near-field geometric context. Second, we pro-
pose a Scalable Flow-based Interaction Policy for partner-conditioned residual
generation. It factorizes dual-humanoid control into a reusable motor founda-
tion and interaction-specific adaptations: partner-aware experts learn corrections
over a frozen whole-body motion prior, while conditional flow matching unifies
them in a shared residual action space. This enables scalable skill consolidation
while retaining pretrained motor capabilities. Extensive experiments demonstrate
that OmniInteract improves retargeting fidelity and dual-humanoid coordination
while better retaining interaction performance as skill diversity grows. Real-world

INTRODUCTION
Advances in whole-body control and motion imitation have equipped humanoid robots with increas-
ingly versatile motor capabilities (Ben et al., 2025; Fu et al., 2024; He et al., 2024). Most existing
systems, however, focus on single-humanoid skills (Chen et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2026; He et al.,
2025; Li et al., 2026b), whereas many real-world tasks require collaboration and physical interaction
between agents (Shaoul et al., 2025; Doshi et al., 2026). Human-human interaction demonstrations
offer a rich source of such behaviors. Transferring them to dual humanoids requires preserving the
intended relationships between the bodies across embodiments and maintaining coordinated motion
and contact under coupled dynamics.
The first challenge is preserving surface interaction geometry during retargeting. Morphological
differences in body proportions and surface shape can disrupt contact interfaces despite plausible
individual poses (Reda et al., 2023; Yin et al., 2025). Although recent interaction-aware approaches
have begun to explicitly model relative geometries between bodies, sparse body representations
remain insufficient for capturing fine-grained surface-level interactions (Chen et al., 2026). Explicit
contact relationships constrain where two bodies should interact, but they do not fully determine how
surrounding limbs and body regions should be arranged (Liu et al., 2025b). Conversely, preserving
neighborhood-level geometric structures may maintain overall spatial consistency while hiding local
contact deviations. Therefore, High-fidelity retargeting requires preserving both where the bodies
meet and how nearby regions are arranged.
The second challenge is retaining coordination fidelity as interaction diversity grows. Independent
motion trackers lack the partner feedback needed to correct deviations under coupled dynamics.
Training from scratch instead requires learning individual tracking, whole-body balance, and partner
coordination together. More importantly, different interactions exhibit substantially different contact
patterns and coordination requirements. As interaction diversity increases, jointly training multiple
interaction behaviors within a unified policy can introduce severe gradient interference, making it
increasingly difficult to maintain high-fidelity interaction performance.
Motivated by this analysis, we propose OmniInteract, a unified framework for learning diverse
dual-humanoid interactions from human demonstrations via surface-aware retargeting and scalable
residual policy learning, as illustrated in Fig. 1. To overcome the retargeting bottleneck, we introduce
Surface Contact-Aware Retargeting (SCAR), which leverages dense human–robot surface corre-
spondences to construct a contact-aware surface interaction graph between humanoid. It preserves
interaction geometry through complementary surface constraints: explicit bilateral contact edges
maintain the contact interface, while near-field Laplacian relationships preserve the surrounding
spatial structures. SCAR jointly optimizes both humanoids under kinematic constraints, generating
synchronized interaction motion references for downstream control.
To overcome the interaction control bottleneck, we propose a Scalable Flow-based Interaction
Policy that consolidates partner-dependent corrections from task-specific dual-humanoid experts
into a unified generative residual controller through flow matching. This design decouples stable
single-humanoid motion priors from interaction control learning, leveraging high-capacity genera-
tive policies to expand interaction representation capability while avoiding severe interference multi-
interaction behaviors reinforcement learning. Extensive experiments demonstrate that OmniInteract
improves retargeting fidelity and dual-humanoid coordination, together with stronger generalization
to unseen motions and better performance retention as interaction diversity grows. Deployment on
two Unitree G1 robots further demonstrates successful contact establishment, stable whole-body
coordination, and recovery from external pushes. Our contributions are summarized as follows:
• We present OmniInteract, a unified framework for learning dual-humanoid interactions from
human demonstrations, integrating interaction-preserving retargeting with scalable coordination.
• We introduce a surface contact-aware retargeting (SCAR) method that leverages dense human–
robot surface correspondences to preserve bilateral contact interfaces and surrounding interaction
geometry for generating synchronized dual-humanoid references.

RELATED WORKS
Motion Retargeting for Humanoid.
Retargeting human motions to humanoid robots is a fun-
damental step for transforming large-scale human demonstrations into robot-executable references
(Park et al., 2025; Luo et al., 2023). Early approaches typically rely on manually designed corre-
spondences between human and robot joints or body parts, followed by optimization under kine-
matic constraints (Araujo et al., 2025; Qin et al., 2022). Recent studies have improved retargeting
quality by incorporating whole-body geometric structures, interaction constraints, and physical fea-
sibility (Yang et al., 2025; Pan et al., 2025). However, existing representations often rely on sparse
body configurations or predefined correspondences, which is insufficient to capture fine-grained sur-
face interactions between multiple humanoids (Chen et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2025b). Recent dense
correspondence-based approaches represent humanoid bodies through surface-level geometric cor-
respondence (Cao et al., 2026), enabling more scalable motion transfer across embodiments. Our
work builds upon this direction and extends surface-based retargeting toward dual-humanoid inter-
action by explicitly modeling bilateral contact interfaces and surrounding interaction geometry.
Whole-body Control for Humanoid Robots.
Learning robust whole-body control policies for
humanoid robots has recently advanced through motion imitation (Ji et al., 2024; Xie et al., 2026;
Han et al., 2025), reinforcement learning (Li et al., 2026a; Peng et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2025),
and generative policy learning (Li et al., 2026c; Liu et al., 2025a; Shao et al., 2025). Prior works
have demonstrated impressive capabilities in single-humanoid locomotion, manipulation (Gu et al.,
2026; He et al., 2026), and dynamic motion tracking (Wang et al., 2026). Extending these abilities
to dual-humanoid interaction introduces additional challenges, as the policy must simultaneously
maintain individual motion stability and adapt to partner-dependent coordination requirements. Ex-
isting dual-humanoid control approaches typically learn interaction policies directly through rein-
forcement learning or task-specific optimization, which can become difficult to scale as interaction
diversity increases (Liu et al., 2025b; Chen et al., 2026). In contrast, our approach leverages pre-
trained single-humanoid motion priors and learns interaction-specific residual adaptations, followed
by specialist-to-unified policy consolidation, enabling scalable learning of diverse dual-humanoid
interactions while preserving stable individual motion capabilities.

OMNIINTERACT
Our goal is to learn whole-body humanoid–humanoid interactions from human–human demonstra-
tions. Unlike independent motion imitation, this task requires preserving inter-body geometry across
embodiments during retargeting and sustaining coordinated behavior under coupled dynamics dur-
ing control. Therefore, we present OmniInteract, a unified framework combines surface contact-
aware retargeting (SCAR) with scalable flow-based interaction policy training to address these
challenges. In this section, we detail (i) how SCAR jointly retargets dual humanoids while pre-
serving contact surface and surrounding spatial geometry, and (ii) how our flow-based interaction
policy training specializes partner-aware residual corrections and consolidates them into a unified
interaction policy. Fig. 2 illustrates the overall pipeline of our framework.
3.1
SURFACE CONTACT-AWARE RETARGETING
Challenge of Interaction Retargeting.
Conventional motion retargeting methods typically rely
on sparse skeletal or keypoint constraints, which preserve coarse body motion but provide insuffi-
cient supervision for the surface contacts that define close human interactions. For example, in an
embrace, aligning the wrists and torso does not ensure that the palms reach the intended regions
of the partner’s back. The core challenge lies in synthesizing motions that simultaneously preserve
precise inter-body surface contacts and their surrounding local geometry.

Given the interaction motions Ma (a ∈{1, 2}) retargeted by SCAR, our goal is to achieve coordi-

EXPERIMENTS
We present extensive experiments in simulation and on physical robots to evaluate the interaction
retargeting fidelity, control scalability, and real-world executability of our proposed OmniInteract
framework. Our experiments are organized around the following key questions:
• Q1: Interaction-Preserving Retargeting. Can SCAR generate feasible trajectories that better
preserve fine-grained inter-body geometry than prior retargeting methods? (Sec. 4.2)
• Q2: Residual Interaction Learning. Do residual experts built on frozen motion priors improve
dual-humanoid coordination over independent or scratch-trained controllers? (Sec. 4.3)
• Q3: Flow-Based Interaction Scaling. Does scaling a flow-based generative policy improve
multi-interaction control more effectively than conventional MLP controllers? (Sec. 4.4)
• Q4: Real-World Robustness. Can OmniInteract transfer to physical humanoids under pertur-
bations while maintaining accurate contacts and stable whole-body coordination? (Sec. 4.5)

EFFECTIVENESS: PARTNER-AWARE RESIDUAL CONTROL (Q2)
Simulation Evaluation.
To evaluate the effectiveness of partner-aware residual learning in lever-
aging single-humanoid motion priors for coordinated dual-humanoid interaction, we compare Om-

CONCLUSION
In this paper, we present OmniInteract to address the challenges of preserving interaction geome-
try across embodiments and scaling coordinated control across diverse dual-humanoid interactions.
We introduce SCAR, which jointly retargets both humanoids through contact-interface and near-
field context modeling, producing synchronized references that preserve fine-grained surface rela-
tionships. We further propose a scalable flow-based interaction policy that factorizes control into a
frozen motor prior and partner-dependent residuals and consolidates expert corrections into a unified
generative controller through flow matching, while retaining pretrained motor capabilities. Exten-
sive experiments demonstrate improved retargeting fidelity, higher coordination success, and better
performance retention as interaction diversity grows, with physical deployment validating contact
establishment and recovery from external perturbations. By connecting surface interaction modeling
with scalable residual control, OmniInteract provides an effective route from human demonstrations
to coordinated physical interactions between humanoids.

Two independent temporal 1D CNNs encode the nor-

ences. During training, a diagonal Gaussian is centered on the concatenated corrected actions of

conditioning observations from the estimated states, evaluates its frozen tracker, and integrates the
flow policy to obtain its own 29-dimensional residual. This correction is added to the base action
following Eq. (27), and converted into desired joint positions. The low-level interface publishes
motor targets and proportional–derivative gains at 500 Hz for joint-level tracking.

BASELINE IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS
To validate our contributions, we benchmark our framework against two sets of baselines: retarget-
ing methods (answering Q1) and control policy variants (answering Q2 & Q3).
Retargeting Baselines.
For fair comparison, all baselines use the same source motions and iden-
tical skeletal-scaling preprocessing.
25


Preprint
• GMR (General Motion Retargeting) (Araujo et al., 2025):
A Cartesian-space
optimization-based retargeting method that independently fits each humanoid to the source
motion by minimizing positional and rotational errors of selected body keypoints, such as
the pelvis and end-effectors. Since the two agents are optimized separately, GMR does not
explicitly model inter-agent interaction constraints.
• OmniRetarget (Yang et al., 2025): A topology-aware retargeting method that uses an
Interaction Mesh (I-Mesh) to preserve geometric structure, originally developed for hu-
man–object interaction. In our dual-humanoid setting, we apply OmniRetarget indepen-
dently to each agent. As a result, it preserves per-agent body geometry but does not explic-
itly constrain the relative spatial relationships between the two humanoids.
• PHC (Luo et al., 2023): A sparse correspondence-based humanoid retargeting pipeline
that fits predefined human joints to robot links. We apply it independently to each agent,
preserving individual motion but without explicit inter-agent geometric constraints.
• UMR (Unified Motion Retargeting) (Cao et al., 2026):A dense surface correspondence-
based retargeting method that aligns human and robot exterior point clouds through con-
strained optimization. We apply it independently to each agent, providing fine-grained
body-surface alignment but without explicit pairwise constraints between the two hu-
manoids.
Control Policy Baselines.
We evaluate our scalable flow-based interaction policy training frame-
work by comparing against representative single-agent baselines and ablated variants. All variants
are trained using the same PPO hyperparameters.
• TWIST2 (Ze et al., 2025): An RL-based whole-body motion tracker conditioned on refer-
ence motion and robot proprioception. We initialize from the official checkpoint and adapt
the tracker on our interaction-motion subsets, applying it independently to each humanoid
with its own reference motion. The policy receives neither partner-state information nor
explicit inter-humanoid interaction cues.
• BeyondMimic (Liao et al., 2026): An RL-based motion-tracking framework that trains
a dedicated policy for each reference motion, targeting high-dynamic whole-body track-
ing. In our dual-humanoid setting, we train one tracker per motion and execute the two
corresponding policies independently for the two robots.
• SONIC (Luo et al., 2026): A large-scale universal whole-body motion tracker that uses
a single unified policy to track diverse reference motions, including unseen motions. We
independently run the same tracker for both humanoids with their respective references,
without explicit peer-state or interaction-aware constraints.
• From scratch RL (w/o Residual Learning): Evaluates the contribution of residual in-
teraction learning built upon a pretrained single-agent motion prior. Implementation: The
frozen base tracker and residual decomposition are removed, and a peer-aware policy is
trained from scratch with PPO to directly predict the full control actions for both hu-
manoids. The observation space and interaction rewards remain unchanged.
• Specialist→Unified MLP (w/o Flow Matching): Evaluates the contribution of flow-based
policy consolidation when integrating interaction specialists into a unified controller. Im-
plementation: The residual specialists and DAgger data-collection procedure remain un-
changed, while the conditional flow-matching policy is replaced with a deterministic MLP
that directly regresses the paired residual actions from the same conditioning inputs and
expert targets.

EVALUATION PROTOCOL AND METRIC DETAILS
This subsection details the evaluation protocol and metrics used throughout the paper. We use task-
specific metrics to evaluate retargeting quality and dynamic control performance of our framework,
respectively.
1) Evaluation Protocol
26


Preprint
Motion segmentation and episode length.
For control policy evaluation, to ensure a consistent
evaluation horizon, we divide all reference motions into 10-second clips, corresponding to 500 con-
trol steps and matching the episode length used during training. Motions shorter than 10 seconds
are evaluated in their original duration. Each motion clip is evaluated over 10 independent rollouts,
and metrics are averaged across these trials.
2) Metrics Details
Retargeting Evaluation Metrics.
We evaluate retargeting quality from three complementary as-
pects: physical feasibility, interaction fidelity, and downstream learnability.
• Maximum Penetration Depth (MPD). Measures physical plausibility by reporting the
maximum inter-humanoid penetration depth over each motion sequence. Penetration is
computed using the MuJoCo (Todorov et al., 2012) collision proxy geometries. We first
compute MPD for each sequence and then average it over the evaluation set. Lower values
indicate fewer severe inter-body collisions.
• Contact Interaction Error (CIE). Measures fine-grained contact fidelity using cross-body
surface pairs whose distance in the source motion is within the contact threshold τc = 2 cm.
For each pair, we compute the L2 deviation between the source and retargeted 3D relative
vectors and normalize it by the canonical humanoid body scale sbody = 1.32 m. CIE
is reported as a percentage, with lower values indicating better preservation of contact
distance and direction.
• Near-field Geometry Error (NGE). Measures preservation of the surrounding interaction
geometry using cross-body surface pairs whose source distances lie between τc = 2 cm
and τn = 15 cm. Pairs whose two endpoints are both contact points are excluded. The
L2 deviation between the source and retargeted relative vectors is normalized by sbody and
reported as a percentage. Lower NGE indicates better preservation of near-field spatial
structure.
• Contact F1 (C-F1). Measures region-level contact preservation. A body-region pair is
considered in contact if at least one cross-body surface pair lies within τc = 2 cm. We
compare the source and retargeted contact labels and report the micro-averaged F1 score
over all frames as a percentage.
• Downstream Success Rate (DSR). Serves as a proxy for downstream physical learnability.
We define DSR as the percentage of RL rollouts that successfully execute the retargeted
reference while preserving the interaction structure, requiring CIE < 20% and NGE <
20% relative to the scaled reference.
Control Policy Evaluation Metrics.
We evaluate dual-humanoid control in terms of motion track-
ing, interaction preservation, and overall execution success with respect to the retargeted reference
trajectories.
• Root-relative Mean Per-Joint Position Error (Empjpe).
Measures individual motion-
tracking fidelity as the mean per-joint position error after removing global root translation.
We compute Empjpe over both humanoids and average it over the rollout. Lower values
indicate more accurate whole-body tracking independent of global translational drift.
• Interaction Geometry Error (Einter-geo). Measures how faithfully the executed motion
preserves the contact interfaces and surrounding interaction geometry of the retargeted ref-
erence. We reuse the CIE and NGE definitions above, replacing the source–retargeted com-
parison with a reference–execution comparison. Contact and near-field surface pairs are de-
termined exclusively from the retargeted reference using the same thresholds and exclusion
rules. For each component, relative-vector deviations are averaged over all corresponding
frame–pair instances in the rollout, normalized by sbody and reported as a percentage.
• Success Rate (Succ.). Measures overall interaction execution success. A rollout is con-
sidered successful when Empjpe < 10 cm, Einter-geo < 20%, and neither humanoid falls or
terminates early. We report the percentage of successful rollouts over all evaluation trials.
