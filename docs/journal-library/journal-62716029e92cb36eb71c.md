## Abstract

Aim: The inspections of the rebar are attempted manually at the point of repetition, posture production that differs in terms of accuracy and throughput. This study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of integrating industrial exoskeletons (both passive and powered) with edge-deployed computer vision systems in improving safety, productivity, and measurement accuracy during rebar inspections. Methods: Thirty qualified investigators participated in completing a randomized 2x2 crossover study, with three projects involving the exoskeleton and vision assist on/off. Participants performed manual and exoskeleton-assisted rebar inspections using vision-augmented tools. Data were collected using surface electromyography (sEMG) and inertial measurement units (IMUs) to assess ergonomic effects. Measurement accuracy and productivity were quantified using precision/recall scores and throughput metrics. Results: Exoskeleton-plus-vision improved throughput, increasing it by 142.174 m² by an average of +22.5 %, and reducing cycle time per 100 m² by 18.2%, based on 1,920 bay-level observations. Erector-spinae and anterior-deltoid activity decreased 3% and 31% respectively, at the same output and time, at near extension beyond 30°, and length of time. Fidelity to measurement was preserved or was improved: spacing MAE was not inferior (0.2 mm vs. the manual), lap error was reduced to 5.1 mm (6.8 mm vs. 2.9 mm), and cover error was reduced to 2.5 mm (2.9 mm vs. 2.5mm. There were increases in safety (near-miss IRR = 0.74) and a reduction in cost per square meter (down to 0.36), resulting in an 18.2% decrease, with a typical payback of 8-13 months, according to device classification. These findings suggest a strategy for safer and faster rebar QA along the auditable line. Conclusion: The study concludes that integrating industrial exoskeleton support with edge-deployed computer vision systems can significantly enhance safety, efficiency, and information traceability during rebar inspection. This makes it a viable and scientifically tested avenue for more automated and ergonomically oriented quality control within the construction industry. Recommendation: It also suggests that construction companies should test and deploy exoskeleton-aided, vision-enabled inspection tools under various site and environmental conditions to optimize deployment standards, verify scalability, and measure the economic benefits of different projects.
