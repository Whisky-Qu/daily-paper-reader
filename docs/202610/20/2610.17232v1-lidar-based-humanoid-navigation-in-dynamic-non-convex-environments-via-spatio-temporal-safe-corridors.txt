# LiDAR-based Humanoid Navigation in Dynamic, Non-Convex Environments via Spatio-Temporal Safe Corridors 

Shubham S. Kumbhar and Panagiotis Artemiadis ∗, IEEE Senior Member 

Abstract —Navigating humanoid robots through dynamic, non-convex environments requires tightly coupling onboard percep-tion with footstep planning and dynamic locomotion control. Existing methods frequently discard complex free space through simplified convex obstacle bounds and fail to account for real-time dynamic occupancy predictions. This paper presents a real-time framework for humanoid navigation that directly constructs spatio-temporal safe corridors from LiDAR-based particle rep-resentations and couples them with Linear Inverted Pendulum (LIP)-based planning and Whole Body Control (WBC) to enable navigation around and within moving non-convex structures without restrictive geometric assumptions. Simulations on the Digit humanoid yield a 96% success rate in navigating around and inside moving non-convex obstacles in a 24 × 24 m 2 arena. Ablation studies confirm that the proposed iterative polytope generation significantly outperforms single-iteration baselines in narrow corridors, while evaluations demonstrate robustness to clutter, sensor noise, control delays, and force perturbations. 

Index Terms —Dynamic occupancy mapping, model predictive control, obstacle avoidance, safe corridors 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Humanoid robots have the potential to operate alongside humans in cluttered, dynamic environments, yet autonomous navigation remains challenging for bipedal systems. Unlike wheeled platforms, bipedal robots regulate their center-of-mass (CoM) dynamics through discrete foot contacts, coupling collision avoidance with the selection and timing of feasible footsteps. Model-based approaches, particularly reduced-order model-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Quadratic Programming (QP), have enabled dynamically stable bipedal locomotion [1–4], motivating their use in obstacle-aware nav-igation. Static-environment navigation was explored in [5] using half-plane constraints in MPC, assuming known obstacle geometry and position. An end-to-end quadruped navigation framework in [6] combined vision-based mapping and global 

A∗ planning with walking and jumping over static obstacles, but did not address dynamic obstacle avoidance. Safe-corridor-based navigation was explored in [1], which incorporated dynamic obstacle avoidance constraints into MPC but assumed simplified convex obstacle geometries and direct availability of obstacle position and velocity information.

The advancements associated with static obstacle navigation have naturally motivated extensions to dynamic environments, where obstacle motion must also be considered. A perception-to-walking pipeline for the humanoid Lola using Swept-Sphere-Volume (SSV) primitives [7] was developed in [8] and later extended with hierarchical footstep replanning [9]. While this representation can capture non-convex obstacles, the framework operates reactively without explicitly predicting obstacle motion. Similarly, a reactive avoidance scheme using spherical obstacle representations was employed in [10]. MPC-QP-based avoidance was extended to fast-moving objects in [11] using half-plane constraints and velocity-stretched ellipsoids for obstacles. However, that approach treats these ellipsoids as static over the MPC horizon rather than incorpo-rating time-varying obstacle predictions, and assumes a priori availability of obstacle states. Predictive MPC-QP obstacle avoidance using half-space relaxations of predicted obstacle motion was introduced in [12]. This approach was extended to bipedal standing and stepping avoidance in [13]. However, both approaches rely on simplified obstacle geometries and externally provided obstacle states. Additionally, these methods are not designed for global navigation and may get trapped in local minima. Social Zonotope Network (SZN)-MPC was proposed in [14] for avoiding dynamic obstacles in social navigation scenar-ios. Specifically, it predicts pedestrian motion using convex zonotopes for MPC-based collision avoidance. However, this representation primarily captures motion uncertainty rather than the physical geometry of obstacles. A learning-based approach, the reactive Agile But Safe (ABS) framework, was proposed in [15] for high-speed quadrupedal locomotion, while the Reflexive Evasion Robot (REBot) framework was proposed in [16] for high-speed evasion. Both approaches remain primarily local and rely on simplified sensing or obstacle representations, limiting their applicability to long-horizon navigation and explicit geometry-aware safety. Together, the existing approaches reveal two key limitations. First, obstacle geometry is often simplified using spheres or el-lipsoids, which can conservatively eliminate free space around non-convex obstacles. This can fundamentally restrict achiev-able navigation tasks. For example, a convex approximation of a C- or U-shaped obstacle may classify its concave interior as occupied, preventing a humanoid from navigating within the enclosed region. Second, most model-based approaches assume that obstacle geometry, position, and/or velocity are al-ready available. Consequently, only a few approaches connect arXiv:2610.17232v1 [cs.RO] 12 Oct 2026 2

onboard spatial sensing and dynamic occupancy prediction with geometry-aware free-space construction, footstep selec-tion, and dynamically stable locomotion. Dynamic obstacle avoidance has been explored more ex-tensively in aerial robotics, where full-stack perception, pre-diction, planning, and control systems have been developed. Particle-based dynamic occupancy representations such as the Dual-Structure Particle (DSP) map enable complex obstacle geometries to be represented while maintaining a lightweight structure suitable for real-time prediction. It was utilized in [17] to construct risk-aware spatio-temporal (RAST) safety corridors for drone navigation. However, its rectangular corri-dor representation can remain conservative around complex obstacle boundaries and does not account for the discrete footstep selection and dynamic stability constraints inherent to bipedal locomotion. In this work, we present an algorithmic systems frame-work that brings the advantages of particle-based dynamic occupancy representations to bipedal navigation and tightly integrates them with footstep planning and locomotion control. The primary contributions are: (i) a full-stack perception-to-control framework for humanoid navigation that bridges 3D Light Detection and Ranging (LiDAR) perception and dynamic locomotion control through a modified DSP-map representation, A* global guidance, capture-region-constrained Linear Inverted Pendulum (LIP) sampling, LIP-MPC, and WBC, enabling collision-free navigation in dynamic, non-convex environments; (ii) a spatio-temporal safe corridor generation method that uses capture-region-constrained LIP trajectory sampling to extract kinematically and dynamically feasible seed points and a risk-aware iterative polytope gen-eration method with per-cluster risk thresholds to construct large, temporally consistent convex free regions from predicted particle-based obstacle occupancy; and (iii) comprehensive simulation-based validation of the complete framework on the Digit humanoid in dynamic, non-convex environments, including navigation within moving non-convex obstacles and robustness to sim-to-real imperfections such as sensor noise, control delays, and external perturbations. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Sec-tion II details the 360 ◦ LiDAR perception and the modi-fied DSP-map. Section III presents the planning and control pipeline, covering A*-based global guidance, LIP trajectory sampling, safe-polytope generation, and MPC-WBC execu-tion. Section IV provides simulation results and Section V concludes the paper. II. P ERCEPTION MODULE

The overall pipeline consists of the following sequential components: A* global path planning, LIP-based footstep sampling for seed extraction, iterative spatio-temporal safe polytope generation, desired velocity computation, LIP-MPC for footstep planning, and WBC for torque computation. A. A* Global Path Planning 

We utilize A* search [21] to compute a collision-free path for local goal guidance in downstream planners. We construct a binary occupancy grid from the LiDAR point cloud mea-surements. This grid is further used for planning a collision-free, low-cost path. We additionally penalize immediate local deviations from the previously computed path to avoid sudden changes in the direction of motion and fluctuations in the local plan. 

1) Grid Construction and Inflation: Given the sensor posi-tion xs ∈ R2, we construct a 20 ×20 m grid G with a resolution of rgrid = 0 .2 m, centered at xs. The grid is populated using the current LiDAR point cloud measurements, with a value of 1 assigned to occupied cells and 0 to free cells, thus forming a binary occupancy grid. Furthermore, the occupied voxels in the grid are inflated by a safety margin of Rinflate = 1 .0 m.

IV. R ESULTS 

We evaluate the proposed framework in dynamic environ-ments containing static and dynamic non-convex obstacles and human actors performing random walks 3. The humanoid is tasked with navigating to a goal located within a moving U-shaped object. This goal-containing object and other dynamic obstacles move at constant velocities. Unlike approaches that approximate non-convex obstacles using convex represen-tations, the DSP-map-based framework does not impose a predefined obstacle geometry, enabling navigation both around and within non-convex obstacles. In all experiments, the goal position within the non-convex region is assumed to be available in the humanoid’s local frame, decoupling high-level semantic goal specification from motion planning and control. Experiments are conducted in MuJoCo [25] using Agility Robotics’ Digit humanoid [26], equipped with a Velodyne VLP-16 LiDAR. LiDAR observations are generated at afrequency of 20Hz through kinematic simulation of Digit in Gazebo [27], with Gaussian measurement noise, control delays, and motor dynamics incorporated to better reflect real-world operation. Figure 2(a) shows representative snapshots of the humanoid navigating successfully among static and dy-namic non-convex obstacles, along with the generated spatio-temporal safe corridors shown in Fig. 2(b).

B. Narrow Corridor: Iterative vs. Non-Iterative Polytope Gen-eration 

We conduct an ablation study in 5 m-long corridors of varying widths to evaluate risk-aware iterative polytope gener-ation. We compare the proposed iterative method with a single polytope-generation iteration as a non-iterative baseline. Ten trials are conducted per corridor width, and success is defined as reaching the opposite end without collision or falling. As shown in Fig. 2(d), both methods perform comparably in wide corridors, but the non-iterative method degrades sharply as the corridor narrows. With the conservative 1 m robot-size assumption, the iterative method successfully traverses corridors as narrow as 1.9 m. The non-iterative baseline retains low-weight particles as active constraints, resulting in more half-space constraints and unnecessarily smaller safe 8

regions. Moreover, its polytopes exhibit abrupt temporal shape changes when the robot is near obstacles. In contrast, the iterative method selectively removes anchors while respecting the cluster-level risk threshold and minimizes the number of active half-spaces per obstacle. This produces larger and more consistent free regions, enabling successful navigation through narrower regions. 

C. Computational Performance 

Figure 2(e) summarizes the per-module computation times for successful experiments across the three arena scales. The DSP-map generation and planning and control modules run in separate threads, enabling fast motion replanning. For all experiments in the results section, 1000 LIP trajectories are sampled for seed-point extraction. The low sampling time is due to the reduced sampling space imposed by the capture-region constraints. The computation times for the planning and control modules remain small and relatively consistent across the experimental conditions, with a modest increase in polytope computation in the 8 × 8 m arena due to its higher obstacle density. We also observe a modest increase in DSP-map update time as clutter density increases. The complete system runs on a desktop equipped with an Intel i9 CPU and 64 GB of RAM, without GPU acceleration. V. C ONCLUSION 

This paper presented an integrated, real-time perception-to-control framework for humanoid navigation in dynamic envi-ronments with non-convex obstacles. The architecture bridges 

360 ◦ LiDAR perception and locomotion control by unifying a modified DSP map, A∗ global guidance, capture-region-constrained LIP sampling, iterative safe corridor generation, LIP-MPC, and WBC. The key algorithmic contribution is a spatio-temporal polytope construction that combines capture-region-constrained LIP sampling with an iterative polytope generation algorithm. By pruning dynamically unrecoverable states early via M -step capture regions, the sampler effi-ciently extracts feasible seed points with few samples. The iterative polytope generation algorithm uses these seed points to construct spatially and temporally consistent convex free regions for future times without restrictive shape assump-tions. Extensive simulation experiments on the Digit humanoid demonstrate a 96% success rate for the 24 m × 24 m arena, validating the framework’s ability to navigate both around and inside moving non-convex obstacles.Ablations confirm the iterative polytope’s superiority in narrow corridors, while evaluations show resilience to sensor noise, delays, and force perturbations.
